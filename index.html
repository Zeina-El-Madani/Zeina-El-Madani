About Me

As a Digital Media Studies major, I believe in story-telling as a tool for change. With time, I came to realize that that one way people are uprooted is by making them forget their roots and origins.

So, one form of resistance is to document and raise awareness for preserving a culture. I believe that giving people a voice and passing on their stories is a powerful way of keeping them alive.

I aspire to be involved in meaningful work that will raise awareness, fight for justice and pressure policymakers to adopt positive change with the hopes that one day we will live in a world where people are not oppressed and stigmatized.

My Mission

Using storytelling as a tool for change, raising awareness, and fighting for justice through documentary filmmaking and photo-documentaries.

My Focus

Sumud Project

Censorship: A Short Experimental Video

Experimental Film Social Justice Political Commentary Metaphorical Storytelling

Project Summary

My project is a short film that critiques censorship and the suppression of free speech within American universities—institutions where the right to expression should be paramount. It highlights the impact of genocide on Palestinian land and people, using the destruction of a pomegranate as a metaphor for the censorship faced by Palestinians and their supporters. This work was created for DMS 103: Essential Digital Media Toolkit at the University of Rochester. The final piece is a 1-2 minute metaphoric video, shot on a Canon C90 camera.

My Role and Duration

I was the sole creator, responsible for the entire production process from pre-production (storyboarding) through filming to post-production (editing). I cast a Palestinian student from the University of Rochester to ensure an authentic voice was centered, allowing her to express her personal experience with the university’s reactions. To create a genuine space for her expression, I provided minimal direction, focusing instead on communicating my camera angles and shot setup. The film was edited and finalized using Adobe Premiere Pro.

Creative Problem

This project was a direct response to the American news media's portrayal of the genocide in Palestine, the public reaction, and, most critically, the response of US universities to student protests against the war and genocide. I aimed to highlight the experience of Palestinians and supportive students who were villainized by their own institutions. The video serves as a metaphoric exploration of the oppression faced by Palestinians and the trauma inflicted by genocide and colonization.

Goals

My goal was to foster an understanding of the experience of being villainized and neglected in the face of injustice and censorship. The film is designed not only to generate sympathy and comprehension but also to compel viewers to advocate for what is right—for justice and peace. It is a call to action, urging everyone to stand against entities, especially educational institutions, that prioritize profit, reputation, and business interests over a humanitarian crisis.
